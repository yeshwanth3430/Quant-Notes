1. The lecture in five questions

Words used here

Nullspace $N(A)$ = the collection of all lists $x$ with $Ax = 0$ (every row adds up to 0). Elimination = subtract a multiple of one row from a row below to make zeros. Back substitution = solve the bottom equation first, then go up. Pivot = the first non-zero number of a row after elimination. Free variable = an unknown with no pivot in its column, which you may choose. Rank $r$ = how many pivots. $n$ = how many columns (unknowns). Reduced form $R$ = the tidiest staircase (pivots 1, zeros above and below). $F$ = the numbers of $R$ in the pivot rows and free columns. $I$ = identity (1s on the diagonal, 0s elsewhere).

Lecture 07 defined the nullspace $N(A)$ = all $x$ with $Ax = 0$. This lecture answers: how do we actually compute it? The answer is an algorithm that works for any matrix, square or rectangular.

#QuestionShort answer
1How do we find all solutions of $Ax = 0$?Elimination, then back substitution with the free variables set to 1s and 0s.
2What if a pivot spot is 0 and nothing below helps?Don't stop: that column is free. Move to the next column.
3What is the rank?The number of pivots = the number of truly independent equations.
4How many special solutions?One per free column: $n - r$ (columns minus rank).
5What is the reduced form $R$ for?It makes the special solutions readable at a glance: $N = \begin{bmatrix} -F \\ I \end{bmatrix}$.
The story behind the whole lecture

Think of the columns of $A$ as products you can trade, and the rows as scenarios (for example Nifty expiry levels). $Ax = 0$ asks: which mixes of products pay exactly nothing in every scenario? Such a mix exists only when some products are copies of others. This lecture is the systematic way to find every copy and every zero-payoff mix.

2. The example matrix, and what to expect

Words used here

Matrix = a table (rows × columns). Row = one equation (one scenario). Column = one unknown's numbers (one product). Unknown $x_j$ = how much of column $j$ we use. $Ax = 0$ = "find amounts that make every row add to zero". Solution = amounts that work. Nullspace = the collection of all solutions.

Three equations, four unknowns (3 rows, 4 columns):

$$ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 & 6 & 8 \\ 3 & 6 & 8 & 10 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad Ax = 0 \iff \begin{aligned} x_1 + 2x_2 + 2x_3 + 2x_4 &= 0 \\ 2x_1 + 4x_2 + 6x_3 + 8x_4 &= 0 \\ 3x_1 + 6x_2 + 8x_3 + 10x_4 &= 0 \end{aligned} $$

Two things to spot before any work

Look atCheckSo
the columnscolumn 2 $= (2, 4, 6)$, and $2 \times$ column 1 $= 2 \times (1, 2, 3) = (2, 4, 6)$ ✓column 2 points the same way as column 1: it adds nothing new
the rowsrow 1 + row 2 $= (1+2,\ 2+4,\ 2+6,\ 2+8) = (3, 6, 8, 10) =$ row 3 ✓equation 3 is just equation 1 + equation 2: not a new condition

Elimination will discover both facts on its own, which matters for big matrices where you can't spot them by eye.

How many solutions to expect?

4 unknowns but at most 3 conditions (really only 2, since equation 3 is a copy). Fewer real conditions than unknowns means there is room left over: infinitely many solutions. The job is to describe them all neatly.

3. Elimination on a rectangular matrix

Words used here

Elimination = subtract a multiple of one row from a row below to create zeros. Pivot = the first non-zero entry used to clear the numbers below it. Multiplier = (number to remove) ÷ (pivot). Echelon form $U$ = staircase shape with zeros under the pivots. Zero row = a row that became all zeros because it was a copy of other rows.

Same method as Lecture 03, with one new rule: if a column has no usable pivot, skip it and keep going. We go one small move at a time. Each step says what we do, why, how (the actual numbers), and what we have now.

Step 1: clear column 1

  1. What we do Look at column 1. Pick the top number, the $1$ in row 1, as our leader (pivot).

    Why We want zeros under the leader. Then rows 2 and 3 no longer contain $x_1$, and a row with fewer unknowns is easier to solve.

    What we have now Leader $= 1$. Under it sit a $2$ (row 2) and a $3$ (row 3). Both must become $0$.

    Why this amount Ask: "how many leaders make 2?" $2 \div 1 = 2$. So we take away 2 copies of row 1 from row 2.

    How First make 2 copies of row 1: $2 \times (1, 2, 2, 2) = (2, 4, 4, 4)$. Then take them away, box by box:

    Why this amount "How many leaders make 3?" $3 \div 1 = 3$. Take away 3 copies of row 1.

    What we have now Column 2 is marked "free". We stay in row 2 and move right to column 3.

    What a skipped column means

    A column with no pivot is a combination of the earlier columns. It brings no new direction. In the story: that product is a copy of products already on the list (here, "2 lots of product 1").

  1. What we do Row 2, column 3 holds a $2$. It is not zero, so it becomes leader number 2.

    Why It is the first non-zero number in row 2, reading left to right.

    What we have now Leader $= 2$. Under it (row 3, column 3) sits another $2$, which must become $0$.

    Why this amount "How many leaders make 2?" $2 \div 2 = 1$. Take away 1 copy of row 2.

    What we see Row 3 is all zeros now, and there is no row below it.

Definition: Echelon form (staircase form)

$U$ is in echelon form: each pivot sits to the right of the pivot above it, and everything below the "staircase" is zero. Some steps are wider than one column (here the step from column 1 jumps to column 3), so it is not a neat triangle.

Where the zero row came from

Row 3 of $A$ was row 1 + row 2. Elimination subtracted exactly those multiples and was left with nothing: a zero row means "this equation was a copy". It says $0 = 0$, always true, so it adds no condition.

4. Rank, pivot columns, free columns

Words used here

Pivot = first non-zero entry of a row in $U$. Rank $r$ = how many pivots. Pivot column = a column that got a pivot; its unknown is a pivot variable (forced). Free column = a column with no pivot; its unknown is a free variable (you choose it). $n$ = number of columns (unknowns).

Definition: Rank

The rank $r$ of a matrix is the number of pivots. Here $r = 2$.

The rank is the most important number about a matrix. It says three equivalent things:

Rank $r = 2$ means…Here
number of independent equations3 equations, but only 2 are new (row 3 = row 1 + row 2)
number of independent columns4 columns, but only 2 bring new directions (columns 1 and 3)
in the story: number of truly different productsthe other 2 products are copies

Label every column

  1. What we do Count the leaders (pivots) in $U$.

    How Row 1 has the boxed $1$: that is 1. Row 2 has the boxed $2$: $1 + 1 = 2$. Row 3 is all zeros: no leader, still $2$.

    What we have now Rank $r = 2$.

  2. What we do Go through the columns one by one and ask "does this column hold a leader?"

    Why A column with a leader gives a pivot variable (forced by the equations). A column without one gives a free variable (our choice).

    ColumnHas a pivot?TypeIts unknown is a…

    4nofree columnfree variable $x_4$

    What we have now Pivot variables: $x_1, x_3$. Free variables: $x_2, x_4$.

  3. What we do Count the free variables with a formula, and check it against the table.

    How Every column is either pivot or free. Columns $n = 4$, pivot columns $r = 2$, so free columns $= n - r = 4 - 2 = 2$. The table also shows 2 free columns (2 and 4) ✓.

    CountFormulaHere

    What we have now 2 free variables, so we will build 2 special solutions.

Why "free" and "pivot"
  • Free variables ($x_2$, $x_4$): you may choose any values you like. No equation pins them down.
  • Pivot variables ($x_1$, $x_3$): once the free ones are chosen, the equations force these, by back substitution. Each pivot row controls exactly one pivot variable.

Trading version: you may hold any amount of the copy products; then the amounts of the original products are forced if the total is to pay zero.

5. The special solutions

Words used here

Free variable = an unknown you may set to any value. Pivot variable = an unknown the equations force. Back substitution = solve the bottom equation first, then move up plugging in. Special solution = set one free variable to 1, the other free ones to 0, and solve for the rest. Checking "in the original $A$" = multiply each row of $A$ by the solution and confirm you get 0.

We solve $Ux = 0$ instead of $Ax = 0$ (same solutions, easier to read). The zero row drops out, leaving two equations:

$$ \begin{aligned} x_1 + 2x_2 + 2x_3 + 2x_4 &= 0 &&\text{(row 1 of } U\text{)} \\ 2x_3 + 4x_4 &= 0 &&\text{(row 2 of } U\text{)} \end{aligned} $$
Recipe for a special solution
  1. Pick one free variable and set it to $1$.
  2. Set all other free variables to $0$.
  3. Back-substitute (bottom row first) to find the pivot variables.

Do this once for each free variable.

Special solution 1: $x_2 = 1$, $x_4 = 0$

  1. What we do Choose the free variables: $x_2 = 1$ and $x_4 = 0$.

    Why The recipe: one free variable is $1$, every other free variable is $0$. This one "switches on" column 2 only.

    What we have now Known: $x_2 = 1$, $x_4 = 0$. Unknown: the pivot variables $x_1$ and $x_3$.

  2. What we do Solve the bottom equation first: row 2 of $U$, $2x_3 + 4x_4 = 0$.

    Why bottom first Once $x_4$ is known, it has only one unknown ($x_3$), so we can answer it straight away.

    How Put $x_4 = 0$ in: $4 \times 0 = 0$, so the equation becomes $2x_3 + 0 = 0$, i.e. $2x_3 = 0$.

    How, next "2 times what is 0?" Divide both sides by 2: $x_3 = 0 \div 2 = 0$.

    What we have now $x_3 = 0$.

  3. What we do Go up to row 1 of $U$: $x_1 + 2x_2 + 2x_3 + 2x_4 = 0$.

    How Put in what we know, one term at a time: $2x_2 = 2 \times 1 = 2$; $\ 2x_3 = 2 \times 0 = 0$; $\ 2x_4 = 2 \times 0 = 0$. So the equation becomes $x_1 + 2 + 0 + 0 = 0$, i.e. $x_1 + 2 = 0$.

    How, next "What plus 2 gives 0?" Take 2 away from both sides: $x_1 = 0 - 2 = -2$.

    What we have now $x_1 = -2$.

  4. What we do Put the four numbers together in order $x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4$.

    $$ s_1 = \begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \quad \begin{matrix} \leftarrow \text{found in step 3} \\ \leftarrow \text{chosen in step 1} \\ \leftarrow \text{found in step 2} \\ \leftarrow \text{chosen in step 1} \end{matrix} $$

  5. Check in the original $A$ Multiply each row of $A$ (not $U$) by $s_1$ and add. Every row must give $0$.

    Row of $A$Row · $s_1$Result
    $(1, 2, 2, 2)$$1(-2) + 2(1) + 2(0) + 2(0) = -2 + 2 + 0 + 0$$0$ ✓
    $(2, 4, 6, 8)$$2(-2) + 4(1) + 6(0) + 8(0) = -4 + 4 + 0 + 0$$0$ ✓
    $(3, 6, 8, 10)$$3(-2) + 6(1) + 8(0) + 10(0) = -6 + 6 + 0 + 0$$0$ ✓

    In market words $-2 \times$ column 1 $+ 1 \times$ column 2 $= 0$, i.e. column 2 $= 2 \times$ column 1. The special solution records the copy: "hold 1 of the copy product, sell 2 of the original: pays nothing".

Special solution 2: $x_2 = 0$, $x_4 = 1$

  1. What we do Choose the free variables the other way round: $x_2 = 0$ and $x_4 = 1$.

    Why Now we "switch on" column 4 only, to see how column 4 is copied.

    What we have now Known: $x_2 = 0$, $x_4 = 1$. Unknown: $x_1$ and $x_3$.

  2. What we do Bottom equation first: row 2 of $U$, $2x_3 + 4x_4 = 0$.

    How Put $x_4 = 1$ in: $4 \times 1 = 4$, so $2x_3 + 4 = 0$.

    How, next "What plus 4 gives 0?" Take 4 away from both sides: $2x_3 = 0 - 4 = -4$.

    How, last "2 times what is $-4$?" Divide both sides by 2: $x_3 = -4 \div 2 = -2$. Check: $2 \times (-2) = -4$ ✓.

    What we have now $x_3 = -2$.

  3. What we do Go up to row 1 of $U$: $x_1 + 2x_2 + 2x_3 + 2x_4 = 0$.

    How One term at a time: $2x_2 = 2 \times 0 = 0$; $\ 2x_3 = 2 \times (-2) = -4$; $\ 2x_4 = 2 \times 1 = 2$. So $x_1 + 0 - 4 + 2 = 0$.

    How, next Tidy the plain numbers: $-4 + 2 = -2$. So $x_1 - 2 = 0$.

    How, last "What minus 2 gives 0?" Add 2 to both sides: $x_1 = 0 + 2 = 2$.

    What we have now $x_1 = 2$.

  4. What we do Put the four numbers together in order.

    $$ s_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 0 \\ -2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \quad \begin{matrix} \leftarrow \text{found in step 3} \\ \leftarrow \text{chosen in step 1} \\ \leftarrow \text{found in step 2} \\ \leftarrow \text{chosen in step 1} \end{matrix} $$

  5. Check in the original $A$ Each row of $A$ times $s_2$, added up, must be $0$.

    Row of $A$Row · $s_2$Result
    $(1, 2, 2, 2)$$1(2) + 2(0) + 2(-2) + 2(1) = 2 + 0 - 4 + 2$$0$ ✓
    $(2, 4, 6, 8)$$2(2) + 4(0) + 6(-2) + 8(1) = 4 + 0 - 12 + 8$$0$ ✓
    $(3, 6, 8, 10)$$3(2) + 6(0) + 8(-2) + 10(1) = 6 + 0 - 16 + 10$$0$ ✓

    In market words $2 \times$ column 1 $- 2 \times$ column 3 $+ 1 \times$ column 4 $= 0$, i.e. column 4 $= 2 \times$ column 3 $- 2 \times$ column 1. Column 4 is also a copy, built from columns 1 and 3.

Why the 1s and 0s pattern
  • Setting a free variable to $0$ in one solution and $1$ in the other makes the special solutions genuinely different: no multiple of $s_1$ (which has $x_4 = 0$) can ever have $x_4 = 1$.
  • Setting all free variables to $0$ would force all pivot variables to $0$ too, giving only $x = 0$: no information. That's why exactly one free variable is 1.

6. The whole nullspace

Words used here

Nullspace = every solution of $Ax = 0$. Combination = multiply lists by numbers and add ($c\,s_1 + d\,s_2$). Special solutions $s_1, s_2$ = the basic solutions from section 5. Subspace = a collection you can't leave by adding or scaling; it always contains the zero list.

The nullspace = all combinations of the special solutions $$ x = c \begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} + d \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 0 \\ -2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -2c + 2d \\ c \\ -2d \\ d \end{bmatrix} \quad \text{for any numbers } c, d $$

One special solution per free variable, so $n - r = 2$ of them. Together they fill a 2-dimensional flat piece (a "plane") inside $\mathbb{R}^4$.

Why combinations are solutions

If $As_1 = 0$ and $As_2 = 0$, then $A(c\,s_1 + d\,s_2) = c\,As_1 + d\,As_2 = c \cdot 0 + d \cdot 0 = 0$. So every combination solves $Ax = 0$ (the nullspace is a subspace, Lecture 07).

Why there are no other solutions

Take any solution $x$. Read off its free entries: say $x_2 = c$ and $x_4 = d$. Now look at $c\,s_1 + d\,s_2$: it has the same free entries ($c$ and $d$). Both are solutions with the same free values, and the free values force the pivot values, so the two vectors are identical.

Example: recover c and d from a solution

Is $x = (-8, 3, 2, -1)$ a solution, and which combination is it?

  1. What we do Read the free entries of $x$: entry 2 and entry 4.

    Why In $c\,s_1 + d\,s_2$, entry 2 is $c \times 1 + d \times 0 = c$ and entry 4 is $c \times 0 + d \times 1 = d$. So the free entries are $c$ and $d$.

    How $x_2 = 3$, so $c = 3$. $\ x_4 = -1$, so $d = -1$.

    What we have now $c = 3$, $d = -1$.

  2. What we do Build $c\,s_1 + d\,s_2 = 3\,s_1 - 1\,s_2$.

    How $3 \times (-2, 1, 0, 0) = (-6, 3, 0, 0)$. $\ -1 \times (2, 0, -2, 1) = (-2, 0, 2, -1)$.

    How, next Add box by box: $(-6 - 2,\ 3 + 0,\ 0 + 2,\ 0 - 1) = (-8, 3, 2, -1)$.

    What we have now Exactly the given $x$ ✓. So $x = 3\,s_1 - s_2$.

  3. Check in the original $A$ Row by row, multiply and add:

Market meaning

Every trade that pays nothing in every scenario is a mix of the basic copy trades (the special solutions). If each basic copy trade costs 0 today, every zero-payoff trade costs 0 and there is no arbitrage. So you only need to check $n - r$ trades, not infinitely many.

7. Reduced row echelon form R

Words used here

$U$ = staircase form (zeros below pivots). $R$ (reduced row echelon form) = the tidiest form: pivots equal 1 and zeros above and below each pivot. Identity $I$ = 1s on the diagonal, 0s elsewhere. $F$ = the numbers of $R$ in the pivot rows and free columns. "Allowed move" = one that doesn't change the solutions.

$U$ is good; $R$ is the cleanest possible form. Two extra moves, both allowed because they don't change the solutions:

MoveRuleWhy allowed
1clear the numbers above each pivot (elimination upwards)subtracting a multiple of one equation from another

Move 1: clear above the pivot in column 3

  1. What we do Start from $U$ and look above the second leader (the $2$ in row 2, column 3). Row 1 has a $2$ there.

    $$ U = \begin{bmatrix} \boxed{1} & 2 & 2 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & \boxed{2} & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

    Why In $R$ every leader must be the only non-zero number in its column. Then each pivot variable sits alone in its own equation, and we can read it off without back substitution.

    What we have now The number to remove is the $2$ in row 1, column 3.

  2. What we do Turn that $2$ into $0$ using row 2.

    Why this amount "How many leaders make 2?" $2 \div 2 = 1$. Take away 1 copy of row 2 from row 1.

    How 1 copy of row 2 is $(0, 0, 2, 4)$. Take it away, box by box:

    col 1col 2col 3col 4

    minus 1 × row 2$0$$0$$-2$$-4$

    Careful $2 - 4$ goes below zero: start at 2, step 4 down, land on $-2$.

    What we have now Row 1 $= (1, 2, 0, -2)$. Column 3 now has zeros above its leader.

Move 2: make the second pivot 1

  1. What we do Divide row 2 by its leader, $2$.

    Why In $R$ every leader must be $1$. Dividing a whole equation by the same non-zero number keeps it true ($2x_3 + 4x_4 = 0$ and $x_3 + 2x_4 = 0$ have the same solutions).

    How Box by box: $0 \div 2 = 0$, $\ 0 \div 2 = 0$, $\ 2 \div 2 = 1$, $\ 4 \div 2 = 2$. So $(0, 0, 2, 4) \div 2 = (0, 0, 1, 2)$.

    What we have now Row 2 $= (0, 0, 1, 2)$. Row 1's leader is already $1$, so nothing to do there.

  2. Final picture Leaders boxed, both equal to $1$, zeros above and below them:

    $$ R = \begin{bmatrix} \boxed{1} & 2 & 0 & -2 \\ 0 & 0 & \boxed{1} & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$
Definition: Reduced row echelon form (rref)

$R$ has: every pivot equal to 1; zeros above and below every pivot; any zero rows at the bottom. MATLAB: rref(A). Python: sympy.Matrix(A).rref().

$A$, $U$ and $R$ all have the same nullspace. Every move was a legal operation on the equations.

8. Reading N straight from R

Words used here

$x_{\text{pivot}}$ = the list of pivot variables; $x_{\text{free}}$ = the list of free variables. $F$ = the free part of $R$. $I$ = identity (1s on the diagonal). Nullspace matrix $N$ = the matrix whose columns are the special solutions. Block multiplication = multiply matrices piece by piece, treating each block like a number.

  1. What we do Write $Rx = 0$ as equations, one per non-zero row of $R$.

    How Row 1 of $R$ is $(1, 2, 0, -2)$: $1x_1 + 2x_2 + 0x_3 - 2x_4 = 0$, i.e. $x_1 + 2x_2 - 2x_4 = 0$. Row 2 is $(0, 0, 1, 2)$: $x_3 + 2x_4 = 0$. Row 3 is zeros: $0 = 0$, no condition.

    What we have now Two equations, and each has exactly one pivot variable in it ($x_1$ in the first, $x_3$ in the second).

  2. What we do Move the free variables ($x_2$, $x_4$) to the right side.

    Why Then each pivot variable stands alone on the left: "pivot variable = something made of free variables".

    How In $x_1 + 2x_2 - 2x_4 = 0$, take $2x_2$ away from both sides and add $2x_4$ to both sides.

    $$ x_1 = -2x_2 + 2x_4 $$

    How, next In $x_3 + 2x_4 = 0$, take $2x_4$ away from both sides.

    $$ x_3 = -2x_4 $$

    What we have now The numbers of $F$ ($2, -2$ in row 1; $0, 2$ in row 2) appear with their signs flipped, because they moved to the other side of the equals sign. No back substitution is needed any more: each pivot variable is given directly.

  3. What we do Plug in the two free choices.

    How, for $s_1$ $x_2 = 1$, $x_4 = 0$. Then $x_1 = -2(1) + 2(0) = -2 + 0 = -2$ and $x_3 = -2(0) = 0$.

    How, for $s_2$ $x_2 = 0$, $x_4 = 1$. Then $x_1 = -2(0) + 2(1) = 0 + 2 = 2$ and $x_3 = -2(1) = -2$.

    Special solutionFree valuesPivot values $= -F \times$ freeResult
    $s_1$$x_2 = 1,\ x_4 = 0$$x_1 = -2(1) + 2(0) = -2$; $\ x_3 = -2(0) = 0$$(-2, 1, 0, 0)$ ✓
    $s_2$$x_2 = 0,\ x_4 = 1$$x_1 = -2(0) + 2(1) = 2$; $\ x_3 = -2(1) = -2$$(2, 0, -2, 1)$ ✓

    What we have now The same two special solutions as section 5, found with no back substitution.

The general pattern

Imagine the pivot columns moved to the front (just for thinking). Then $R$ looks like

$$ R = \begin{bmatrix} I & F \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad x = \begin{bmatrix} x_{\text{pivot}} \\ x_{\text{free}} \end{bmatrix} $$

Multiplying by blocks, $Rx = 0$ says

$$ I\,x_{\text{pivot}} + F\,x_{\text{free}} = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x_{\text{pivot}} = -F\,x_{\text{free}} $$

Choose $x_{\text{free}}$ = the columns of the identity (one free variable 1, the rest 0). Then all special solutions appear together as the columns of the nullspace matrix:

$$ N = \begin{bmatrix} -F \\ I \end{bmatrix} $$

Check by blocks: $RN = \begin{bmatrix} I & F \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} -F \\ I \end{bmatrix} = I(-F) + F\,I = -F + F = 0$ ✓.

For our $A$, step by step:

  1. What we do Cut $F$ out of $R$: keep the pivot rows (1 and 2) and the free columns (2 and 4).

    How Row 1, column 2 of $R$ is $2$; row 1, column 4 is $-2$; row 2, column 2 is $0$; row 2, column 4 is $2$.

    $$ F = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -2 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix} $$

  2. What we do Flip every sign of $F$ to get $-F$.

    Why $x_{\text{pivot}} = -F\,x_{\text{free}}$: the numbers moved across the equals sign.

    How $2 \to -2$, $\ -2 \to 2$, $\ 0 \to 0$, $\ 2 \to -2$.

    $$ -F = \begin{bmatrix} -2 & 2 \\ 0 & -2 \end{bmatrix} $$

    What we have now Row 1 of $-F$ gives $x_1$, row 2 gives $x_3$ (the pivot variables).

  3. What we do Put the identity $I = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$ in the free rows (rows 2 and 4 of $N$), and $-F$ in the pivot rows (rows 1 and 3 of $N$).

    Why The free rows hold our choices: column 1 of $N$ has $x_2 = 1, x_4 = 0$; column 2 has $x_2 = 0, x_4 = 1$.

    How Row 1 of $N$ = row 1 of $-F$ $= (-2, 2)$. Row 2 = row 1 of $I$ $= (1, 0)$. Row 3 = row 2 of $-F$ $= (0, -2)$. Row 4 = row 2 of $I$ $= (0, 1)$.

    $$ N = \begin{bmatrix} -2 & 2 \\ 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \qquad (\text{columns} = s_1, s_2) $$

  4. Check in the original $A$ $AN$ must be all zeros. Each box is (row of $A$) · (column of $N$):

    Row of $A$· column 1 of $N$ $= (-2, 1, 0, 0)$· column 2 of $N$ $= (2, 0, -2, 1)$
    $(1, 2, 2, 2)$$-2 + 2 + 0 + 0 = 0$ ✓$2 + 0 - 4 + 2 = 0$ ✓
    $(2, 4, 6, 8)$$-4 + 4 + 0 + 0 = 0$ ✓$4 + 0 - 12 + 8 = 0$ ✓
    $(3, 6, 8, 10)$$-6 + 6 + 0 + 0 = 0$ ✓$6 + 0 - 16 + 10 = 0$ ✓

This is exactly what MATLAB's null(A, 'r') (or the teaching code nulbasis(A)) computes: find $R$, flip the signs of $F$, place the identity in the free rows.

9. Second example: the transpose

Words used here

Transpose $A^T$ = the table flipped so rows become columns (row 1 of $A$ is column 1 of $A^T$). Row swap = exchange two rows when a pivot spot is 0 but a lower row has a non-zero number there. Rank = number of pivots. Free column, special solution, $R$, $F$, $N$: same meanings as in sections 4–8.

Rows of $A$ become columns:

$$ A^T = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 4 & 6 \\ 2 & 6 & 8 \\ 2 & 8 & 10 \end{bmatrix} \quad (4 \times 3) $$

Predict first: column 3 $= (3, 6, 8, 10)$ and column 1 + column 2 $= (1 + 2,\ 2 + 4,\ 2 + 6,\ 2 + 8) = (3, 6, 8, 10)$ ✓. So column 3 should turn out free, and columns 1, 2 pivot: expect rank 2 and $3 - 2 = 1$ special solution.

  1. What we do Set the only free variable to $1$: $x_3 = 1$.

    Why One free variable, so one special solution. There are no other free variables to set to $0$.

In finance
  • Put the payoffs of the products you can trade into the columns of $A$ (rows = Nifty expiry scenarios). A vector in the nullspace is a trade that pays exactly 0 in every scenario.
  • Rank = how many truly different payoff directions the market offers. Free columns = redundant products, each copyable from the pivot products; $F$ in $R$ is the copying recipe.
  • Special solutions = the basic "buy the copy, sell the recipe" trades, one per redundant product. Every zero-payoff trade is a mix of them. Each should cost 0 today; if one doesn't, that is arbitrage. Example 1 does this with Nifty options (CE + PE = 4 bonds, CE − PE = 2 futures).

Example 6: Adding an option with a kink removes a copy (finance)

Problem

In Example 1's market, replace the future with the 20,000 CE, which pays $(0, 0, 1)$ (its strike sits inside the range). What happens to the rank and the zero-payoff trades?

Story first

Straight-line payoffs (bonds, futures, deep-ITM options) can only ever give two directions. An option whose strike is between the scenarios bends: flat, then rising. That bend is a genuinely new direction the others can't copy.

Words used here

20,000 CE = call option with strike 20,000: pays $\max(S - 20{,}000, 0)$, i.e. 0 at 19,900, 0 at 20,000, 100 points (= 1) at 20,100. Kink = the bend where an option's payoff switches from flat to rising. Column = one product; row = one Nifty level. Rank = how many pivots. Free product = column with no pivot (a copy). Special solution = free product 1 lot, then solve. Zero-payoff trade = basket that pays 0 at every level.

$$ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 & 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 3 & 1 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \quad (\text{CE}_{19{,}800},\ \text{PE}_{20{,}200},\ \text{bond},\ \text{CE}_{20{,}000}) $$

  1. What we do Leader = the $1$ at the top of column 1. Clear the $2$ in row 2.

    Why this amount $2 \div 1 = 2$: take away $2 \times (1, 3, 1, 0) = (2, 6, 2, 0)$.

    How $2 - 2 = 0$, $\ 2 - 6 = -4$, $\ 1 - 2 = -1$, $\ 0 - 0 = 0$.

    What we have now Row 2 $= (0, -4, -1, 0)$.

  2. What we do Clear the $3$ in row 3.

    Why this amount $3 \div 1 = 3$: take away $3 \times (1, 3, 1, 0) = (3, 9, 3, 0)$.

    How $3 - 3 = 0$, $\ 1 - 9 = -8$, $\ 1 - 3 = -2$, $\ 1 - 0 = 1$.

    What we have now Row 3 $= (0, -8, -2, 1)$.

  3. What we do Leader 2 = the $-4$ in row 2. Clear the $-8$ under it.

    Why this amount $-8 \div -4 = 2$: take away $2 \times (0, -4, -1, 0) = (0, -8, -2, 0)$.

    How $0 - 0 = 0$, $\ -8 - (-8) = 0$, $\ -2 - (-2) = 0$, $\ 1 - 0 = 1$.

    StepNew row

    row 3 − 3 × row 1$(0, -8, -2, 1)$
    row 3 − 2 × row 2$(0, 0, 0, 1)$

    What we have now Row 3 is not zero this time: the $1$ in column 4 is leader 3. The kinked option's own column saved it.

    $$ U = \begin{bmatrix} \boxed{1} & 3 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & \boxed{-4} & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & \boxed{1} \end{bmatrix} $$

  4. What we do Count leaders and label products.

    How Leaders in columns 1, 2, 4: rank 3. Column 3 (bond) has none: $4 - 3 = 1$ free product.

    In market words Three pivots, rank 3: the market now spans every payoff over the three scenarios. Only the bond is free.

  5. What we do Special solution: bond $x_3 = 1$. Bottom row of $U$ first: $x_4 = 0$.

    Why Row 3 of $U$ is $0 + 0 + 0 + 1x_4 = 0$, which just says $x_4 = 0$.

  6. What we do Row 2 of $U$: $-4x_2 - x_3 + 0x_4 = 0$.

    How Put $x_3 = 1$, $x_4 = 0$: $-4x_2 - 1 = 0$. Add 1 to both sides: $-4x_2 = 1$. Divide by $-4$: $x_2 = -\tfrac14$.

  7. What we do Row 1 of $U$: $x_1 + 3x_2 + x_3 + 0x_4 = 0$.

    How $3x_2 = 3 \times (-\tfrac14) = -\tfrac34$. So $x_1 - \tfrac34 + 1 = 0$. Tidy: $-\tfrac34 + 1 = \tfrac14$, so $x_1 + \tfrac14 = 0$, so $x_1 = -\tfrac14$.

    What we have now $(-\tfrac14, -\tfrac14, 1, 0)$. Scale by 4 for whole lots: $(-1, -1, 4, 0)$, the same "CE + PE = 4 bonds" trade as before.

Three pivots (columns 1, 2, 4), rank 3: the market now spans every payoff over the three scenarios. Only the bond is free; its special solution is still $(-1, -1, 4, 0)$ (after scaling).
